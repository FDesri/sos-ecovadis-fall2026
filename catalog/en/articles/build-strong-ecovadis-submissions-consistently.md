# How do you keep improving so EcoVadis is not a one-off?

## In brief

**Expert insight — François Dequenne:** building a strong EcoVadis submission is not a one-off task but an ongoing journey that evolves with your organisation. Scores become more competitive every year, as medals are reserved for companies in the top performance percentiles — making what counted as good practice yesterday average tomorrow.

> "EcoVadis isn't a box to tick, but a journey of continuous improvement."

To keep pace, think in terms of two complementary pathways: **strengthening your sustainability management system**, and **executing each assessment with precision**. Over time they blend into a cycle of continuous improvement rather than a rush before the deadline.

## Why EcoVadis matters for your business

A strong rating helps you: develop or strengthen a management system in which policies, action plans and metrics form a coherent whole; benchmark performance over time instead of one-off reporting; ease access to finance by demonstrating that ESG risks are identified, managed and monitored; manage supply chain risk through visibility on supplier practices; and align with global best practice, since the methodology draws on widely recognised international standards. The benefits amplify when the assessment is part of a broader strategy rather than just a customer-driven requirement.

## EcoVadis as a journey, not a checkbox

The journey mindset changes each cycle: every submission becomes an opportunity to refine strategy, documentation and data. It matters because the methodology evolves — criteria become more rigorous, expectations for evidence quality increase, leading practices shift as more companies mature. A submission that once secured Gold might not be enough in future years if the organisation stands still. If the long-term goal is Platinum-level performance, incremental improvements must be woven into business planning, budgeting and governance — not left until the platform opens.

## Pathway 1: strengthen your system before you submit

**Understand where you stand today.** The "gap-first" approach maps existing policies, procedures, KPIs and certifications against the EcoVadis themes; identifies which practices are formalised and which remain informal or undocumented; and clarifies roles and responsibilities to expose ownership gaps. The diagnosis shows both strengths to highlight and areas needing structure before the next assessment.

**Use your results as a planning tool.** Beyond the overall score, break results down: which themes underperform and why; where evidence was missing or too weak; which criteria offer the best near-term opportunities. Then design an improvement roadmap over several cycles rather than fixing everything at once — it guides how resources go to policies, actions, training and data systems.

**Turn insights into a phased roadmap.** Distinguish urgent actions, important ones needing preparation, and longer-term initiatives serving strategic ambitions. The aim: moving from isolated efforts to a coherent, resilient management system that underpins a steadily improving score.

## Pathway 2: execute the current assessment with precision

**Treat the assessment as a project.** Define a clear timeline from platform opening to submission; assign roles across departments; plan reviews and approvals so nothing happens at the last minute. Phases: scoping, data and document collection, drafting, internal review, final approval — a discipline especially useful under tight customer deadlines.

**Collect and qualify evidence.** Replace "send me anything related to sustainability" with targeted requests: a list of required documents linked to specific questions and criteria; a clarified entity scope (sites, business units, geographies) per piece of evidence; documents checked as current, approved and aligned with the relevant period. A well-curated evidence library also improves internal information management and pays off over multiple cycles.

**Craft clear, consistent responses.** Use the Policies-Actions-Results framework to tell the story: state what each document demonstrates (policy, action or result); describe scope, boundaries and timeframe in plain language; keep terminology and data consistent so policies connect to actions and actions to results. Structured presentation helps reviewers award points confidently — and helps internal stakeholders see how their work contributes.

## Combining both pathways over time

In the first years, most organisations lean on execution to answer customer requests quickly; with familiarity, they tackle deeper structural gaps strategically. The most effective run a continuous loop: each cycle delivers a well-evidenced score and clear feedback → feedback drives a structured review of policies, processes and data → improvements show up in the next assessment, raising both performance and confidence. Whether the immediate priority is a first rating or long-term transformation, the journey mindset ensures progress doesn't stop when the questionnaire is submitted.

## Key takeaways

- Medals are percentile-based and the methodology evolves: standing still means falling behind.
- Pathway 1 — diagnose gaps, use results as a baseline, build a phased multi-cycle roadmap.
- Pathway 2 — run the assessment as a project, qualify every piece of evidence, structure responses in P-A-R.
- The two pathways converge into a loop: submission → feedback → structural review → better next submission.
